For `n` agents the explicit family uses `n+1` outcomes: one common outcome
and one private outcome per agent. Set `alpha=epsilon` and
`delta=epsilon^(n+1)`. Each agent places `alpha` on its private outcome,
`delta` on every other private outcome, and the remaining mass on the common
outcome. Full support holds for every `0<epsilon<1/4`.

Theorem 8 supplies the binary impossibility contrast. That contrast is
universally quantified and is handled analytically, not inferred from the
finite grid printed by the verifier.
